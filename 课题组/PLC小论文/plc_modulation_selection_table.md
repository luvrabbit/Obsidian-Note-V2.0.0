# PLC Modulation Selection Comparison (Battery In-Situ / Smart-Cell Context)

This table consolidates modulation choices and *paper-reported* quantitative parameters (carrier/frequency, data rate, BER/error, and implementation notes) from the converted corpus under `/home/luv/plc_refs_txt/`.

| Modulation | Spectral Efficiency (Qualitative) | Hardware Complexity / Size | Cost (Qualitative) | Power (Paper-Reported Examples) | Robustness (Paper-Reported / Qualitative) | Suitability For Battery Modules (Evidence) | Reported Battery-PLC Examples (Frequency / Data Rate / Error)
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OOK (2-level ASK) | Low | Lowest (envelope / comparator demod possible; simple MCU drive) | Lowest | `<100 mW` normal; `~48 mW` without LED; `~150 mW` peak per board (Vincent 2021) | Sensitive to amplitude attenuation/noise; robustness improved via conservative bit timing. Landinger 2020 explicitly flags converter noise as a major risk and suggests FEC/redundancy. | Strong fit when payload is small (cell telemetry) and BOM/power are tight; demonstrated across 21700 and 18650 stacks. | Vincent 2021: TI `THVD8000DDFR`, `5 MHz` carrier, OOK; modem supports up to `500 kbit/s` (RS-485, `10 cycles/bit` rec.), but their RS-232 link limited to `115.2 kbps`; `<0.005%` message error rate. Landinger 2020: OOK at `20.40 MHz`; inferred possible `>3.3 Mbit/s` from `300 ns` switching, but used `500 kbit/s` for verification; `1000` packets, BER derived from CRC reported as `0`; measured attenuation `17 dB` downlink / `7 dB` uplink.
| FSK | Low to Medium | Low to Medium (frequency generation + discriminator/PLL; often more tolerant to amplitude variation) | Low to Medium | Nature 2025 reports chip power `~21.50–21.69 mW` (overall chip, includes sensing + comms) | Generally more tolerant to amplitude attenuation than OOK; Nature 2025 uses “advanced” FSK and reports error rate `~1e-5` while recovering `<1 mV` modulation amplitude. | Good fit when ultra-low data rate is acceptable but high reliability at very small signal amplitude is required (hazard/internal sensing). | Nature 2025: “advanced frequency shift keying”; selected `1,200 bps` baud; modulation amplitude region `<1 mV` recovered with error rate `~10^-5`; wireless receiver link is Sub-1GHz protocol at `0.5 Hz` (external hop), but internal-to-tab transmission is PLC-based.
| QPSK / PSK (incl. modem QPSK) | Medium | Medium (coherent demod; tighter analog/RF design than OOK/FSK) | Medium | Not explicitly reported as a board power number in Hao 2025 text slice; modem tolerance and BER are reported. | Hao 2025 shows stable BER `~10^-6` across `5–30 MHz` with spikes at specific frequencies; modem tolerance cited as `40 dB` attenuation; frequency selection strongly affects group delay/BER (avoid `12.5–15 MHz`). | Strong candidate for “robust band” operation in modules/packs; Hao 2025 gives application-guided sub-bands for CTP vs CTM. | Hao 2025: Yamar `SIG102` EVB, QPSK, adjustable carrier `5–30 MHz`, data rate `115.2 kbps`; BER in `5–30 MHz` reported `1.0×10^-6` to `1.17×10^-6` (no error correction); recommends `5–10 MHz` and `20–30 MHz` sub-ranges; not recommended `12.5–15 MHz` (group delay instability / BER issues). Also reports max observed signal attenuation `~3.3 dB` at `30 MHz`, min attenuation `~0.25 dB` at `5 MHz` (VNA-based channel characterization).
| QAM (high-order) | High (in theory; bits/symbol grows with order) | Highest (linear front-end, tight EVM/phase noise, DSP; sensitive to channel variation) | Highest | Not typically a “single number” in these papers because it depends on RF instrumentation / modem; used mostly as a performance probe. | Strongly frequency- and order-dependent in battery channel; high-order QAM can fail badly at some frequencies. | Good if you *need* higher throughput and can afford complexity; requires careful carrier selection and adaptive order control as pack impedance/SoC/SoH vary. | Koshkouei 2022 (IEEE): QAM tested for carrier `10–200 MHz` (100k symbols, symbol rate `100 kHz`); for `1024-QAM` BER reaches `0.48` at some frequencies (e.g., around `160 MHz`) and stays high in `152–168 MHz`; for `256-QAM` shows a small BER increase `5×10^-6` at `159 MHz`; recommends carrier ranges `40–80 MHz` and `105–148 MHz` for minimum BER/SER across tested configurations. Sensors 2022 (06144/2634 context): QAM investigated up to GHz-range; emphasizes that carrier and SoC/impedance changes can make some combinations unsuitable.
